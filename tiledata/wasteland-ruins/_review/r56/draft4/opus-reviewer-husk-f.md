## husk-f verdict

The building itself is sound. At 1x it clearly reads as a 4-storey grey concrete apartment block: a gravel roof with a lit parapet sits above a south facade with four window rows, balconies with railings and a ground-floor door. There are no side or isometric faces. The palette, window glints and vine all match husk-tall and the modern-city sheet, and nothing in it reads as text or a face. Its footprint is distinct. The roof stair housing correctly shows its top plane plus a front with a door.

It fails on the collapse, which is the point of the piece. Rules 3 and 6 are not met.

1. **The pit does not read as "looking down several floors"** (x≈215–320, y≈0–190). Dark navy filled with jumbled grey shards, none a clear exposed floor plane; at 1x a smeared dark gash or rubble mass, not a stepped drop. Fix: 2–3 clear broken slabs, one per storey, each stepped further down and south-west, each with a 2px light top, 1px dark broken lip, 2–3px darker wall face under it; darkest navy only for the deepest gap near y≈150–190; let one slab show the floor-4 room behind it.
2. **Noisy speckle.** About 15–20 isolated 1px rust/orange dots across the pit and facade break (e.g. (230,45), (290,90), (265,130), (300,215)). Fix: 2–3 deliberate rebar strands hanging off slab lips only.
3. **Regular tick rhythm on the top edge** (x≈225–305, y≈0–20): rust pixels along the broken roof edge at near-even 3–4px spacing (the husk-d fault). Fix: irregular, 1–2 bent stubs; rest a ragged grey lip.
4. **The silhouette is not properly closed** around the pit (x≈300–320, y≈20–190): navy pit pixels touch the background directly. Fix: 1px dark-slate outline along the ragged right and top break.
5. **The spill onto the facade is muddy** (x≈240–320, y≈190–250). Fix: one fallen slab chunk with lit top and dark south face plus 2–3 small chunks.

Nits: roof plane deep (~37%) but acceptable; balcony railings same rhythm, acceptable.

VERDICT husk-f: FAIL
