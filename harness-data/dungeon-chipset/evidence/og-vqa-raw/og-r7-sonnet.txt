VERDICT: FAIL

Judged from the images only. Coordinates: the 12x8 floor is 576x384 at 3x (left half), so a 64px field repeat is 192px at 3x. The field image is 512x512 at 8x. The room scene is 480x384 with the floor at x48-432, y145-335. The edge variants image is 405x303 at 3x, 8 columns x 6 rows, about 48px per cell.

Big improvement over r6: the carpet noise and the 16px per-tile crack stamp are gone. It now reads as cracked slabs, and the construction is much more coherent. The defects below are what remains.

Major defects
1. The 64px field repeat is visible (point 2), major.
   - The violet glow dots sit on an obvious lattice in the 12x8 floor. At y~43 they are at x~96, 288 and 480. At y~72 they are at x~72, 264 and 456. At y~90 they are at x~142, 334 and 526. At y~135 they are at x~48, 240 and 432. At y~180 they are at x~110, 302 and 494. The same set returns exactly 192px right and 192px down.
   - The large light and dark slab clusters repeat too (for example the maroon slab group around x~130-180, y~45-90 returns at x~322 and x~514).
   - In a 12x8 view the eye reads rows and columns of identical glow dots, and it will be worse on a larger map. The glow is the most salient feature, so it exposes the repeat.
   - There is no 16px grid and no tile-border seam. The problem is the 64px repeat and the glow placement on it.
2. Slab interiors are flat fills (point 3), major.
   - In the 8x field, each slab is almost a single flat value with a few sparse 1px specks. Examples: the dark indigo slab at x~210-290, y~30-110 and the maroon slab at x~280-390, y~110-210.
   - There is no bevel, highlight, shadow step or light direction on any slab. The only shading is the dark outline, so each slab reads as a flat sticker with a border. The brief asks for no flat fills and 3+ shading steps.
   - This is clearly less information per slab than Beodeul's organic multi-tone texture.
3. Edges are not shaded and not distinguishable (point 5), major.
   - In the 47 edge variants, all cells show the same dark slab with the same dark crack line running from the left-middle to the lower-right (e.g. row 1 cell 1 at x~5-45,y~10-45, and row 6 cell 3 at x~105-145,y~255-295). They differ mostly by a faint 1px rim. No variant has a visible shaded transition or wall shadow, and the 47 shapes cannot be told apart at 3x.
   - In the room scene the floor meets the brick wall at y~145 with a hard line. The first slab row (y~147-180) is at full brightness, with no gradient shadow under the wall.
4. Inconsistent variants in the room scene.
   - Patches of the floor are much darker and flatter, with almost no crack lines. Examples are x~95-190,y~200-290 around the actor and x~340-385,y~200-250. Elsewhere the slabs are bright, with full dark outlines.
   - The result looks like blotchy dark flat areas laid over the slab field, so the floor does not read as one stone.

Moderate / minor
5. Colour (point 1): about half the slabs are maroon/brown-red (field image: x~100-200,y~0-80; x~280-380,y~120-200). They read as red-brown cobble, not purple-black. The indigo slabs do read as purple-black. The alternation of maroon and indigo slabs also makes the pattern feel like a cobblestone street more than a demon-realm floor.
6. Outline lattice (point 2): the uniform dark teal outline around every slab (about 1-2px wide everywhere) forms a continuous network. It does not read as mortar and has no tile-aligned seams. But its constant width and dark colour make it a strong graphic pattern, and it looks the same on all slabs.
7. Glow (point 4): the violet cracks are bright, thick (3-4px), blocky, and the same brightness everywhere. They read as a handful of glow highlights and not as faint light in cracks. They have no falloff or halo, and each one is a straight short bar. The brief asks for "faint" glow in "some" cracks.
8. Cracks (point 4): the crack network is the slab outline, so there is no secondary cracking. Only a few slabs have inner hairline cracks (field x~60-90,y~150-170 and x~370-400,y~310-330).

Quality vs Beodeul (point 6)
- Beodeul volcano (right half of the 12x8 image) is organic, dark and low-chroma, with a mid-scale patchy texture and no visible repeat.
- The candidate is cleaner and more legible as stone, and clearly better than r6. It is more saturated, flatter per slab, and has a visible repeat. It is still below the Beodeul bar for shading steps and information per slab.

What would likely fix it: break the 64px repeat (glow placement and slab-colour clusters, e.g. a larger field or a few alternating fields), add 2-3 shading steps with top-left light on slabs, shade the first row under walls and the edge variants, and darken or purple-shift the maroon slabs.
