c01 (tall brick gabled townhouse, crowstep gable)

A. 3/4 view: The roof is a pentagon-shaped gable end split down a vertical ridge into a bright orange left half and a dark maroon right half. This reads as two sideways roof faces lit from different directions (isometric-style side planes), not as one upper roof surface seen from above. The white crowstep line runs diagonally on top of the facade and the ridge is drawn as a vertical pole. The roof's top surface depth is shallow compared with the Beodeul reference, whose roof is a wide, clearly visible upper plane. Only the south facade is otherwise visible, which is correct, but the roof breaks the required view.
B. Structure: No grass slits or gaps through the building at 4x. The bottom meets the ground. One main door (arched, bottom). The upper floors have three extra shuttered/hoist openings (loading hatches) that could be read as extra doors, which is ambiguous. The thin pole on the ridge and the hanging barrel/hoist stub above the gable read as floating fragments.
C. Style: Brick facade has decent stepped shading, window sills and shutters are well done. The roof halves are two very different hues (orange vs maroon) with sparse bright speckles, which does not harmonise with the Beodeul roofs. The outer outline on the roof and walls is thin or absent rather than a clear dark material-toned outline. The facade is proportioned as a tall narrow tower and looks like a flat front elevation rather than a Beodeul-style massed building.

MAJOR:
- Roof reads as two sideways isometric faces (left lit, right shadow) split by a vertical ridge, violating the 3/4 requirement of a single visible upper roof surface.
- Floating ridge pole and hoist stub on top of the gable, plus hatches that can be read as extra doors.
- Roof colours and outline do not match the Beodeul references.

VERDICT: FAIL
