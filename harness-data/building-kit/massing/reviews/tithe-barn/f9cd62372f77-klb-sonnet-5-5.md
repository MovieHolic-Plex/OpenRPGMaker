c05 observations (long stone hall with two-tier roof and gabled porch)

A. 3/4 view: There are two stacked roof planes, an upper and a lower skirt. Both show real tile surfaces, with hip edges at the left and right ends. This is more than a thin strip. Only the south facade is visible. The central gabled porch roof is seen from above with both slopes. There are no isometric side faces.

B. Structure: I inspected the 4x board top to bottom. A dark eave strip runs continuously between the upper and lower roof and I found no grass band between the tiers. The roof meets the wall course with no gap. The bottom edge meets the ground. There is exactly one door (the double-leaf timber door in the porch, which counts as one door). Nothing is floating or garbled. The shutters and windows are readable.

C. Style: The roof tiles vary in several tones, with highlights and dark flecks. The pink and white stone wall has varied course widths and quoin blocks. Outlines are dark and tonal, not black. The palette matches the Beodeul terracotta roofs and the stone and timber details. Weaknesses: the lower roof band is a little busy, and the wall is somewhat uniform. Both are minor and not blocking.

MAJOR: none
VERDICT: PASS
