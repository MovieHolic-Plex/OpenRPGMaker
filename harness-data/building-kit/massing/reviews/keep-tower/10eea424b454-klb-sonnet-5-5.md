c07 observations (stone tower with rooftop terrace and small cottage)

A. 3/4 view: The terrace top is seen from above as a stone floor with a back parapet and side parapets, and the small cottage on it has a hip roof whose upper surface and tile courses are visible. Only the south facade of the tower is shown, with a corbelled battlement band between terrace and shaft. No isometric side faces. The tower is drawn flat-fronted like the Beodeul tower reference, not as a curved cylinder, which is acceptable for the reference style.

B. Structure: I inspected the 4x board top to bottom. No band of grass or background shows through the building. The terrace overhang joins the shaft through the dark machicolation band with no gap. The bottom edge meets the ground with a base ledge. There are two doors. The arched timber door with a gold cross at the base of the tower is one. The cottage on the terrace has its own timber door (about x 133, y 150 on the 4x board). The rule is exactly one door, so this is a second door. The lower window slit at about x 250, y 420 is a dark blob and reads slightly garbled next to the cleaner slit above it, which is a minor point.

C. Style: The stone has several tones, varied block sizes, highlights and dark joints, and it is comparable to the Beodeul tower. Outlines are dark and tonal. The red tile roof of the cottage varies in tone and fits the palette. The barrel and crates are readable. The terrace floor is a bit uniform. These are minor points. The scale of the cottage against the tower door is plausible.

MAJOR: Two doors, one arched door at the tower base and a second timber door on the rooftop cottage, which violates the exactly-one-door rule.
VERDICT: FAIL
