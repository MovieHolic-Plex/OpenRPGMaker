# c01 (stone church with bell tower and gabled porch)

A. 3/4 view: The nave roof shows a real upper surface (about 37px tall at 1x against about 30px of wall), and only the south facade is visible. The tower has a crenellated parapet with a cone above it, which reads well. Weak points: the porch gable's roof wedge overlaps the nave roof surface with no clear ridge or valley, so its relation to the nave roof is ambiguous. The nave roof is a plain rectangle with no ridge line and no eave overhang, so its depth is hard to read next to the Beodeul roofs.

B. Structure: There are no transparent holes, and the walls meet the ground. The porch sticks out below the nave line, which is acceptable. Two pale vertical shafts in the nave wall (around x=390 and x=455 at 4x) have no capitals or bases and look washed-out and semi-transparent against the wall. They cannot be read as buttresses, columns or pipes, so they look garbled. There is one door, in the porch.

C. Style: The stone courses on the tower are well shaded, and the outline tones are mostly right. The roof, however, is a visibly repeated stamp. The same "F"-shaped highlight tile is repeated on a regular grid across the whole nave roof, and the Beodeul roofs vary far more. The porch wall and nave wall have large light areas with little stipple. The roof's top edge is a flat dotted line rather than a dark outline in the material's tone.

MAJOR:
- The two unreadable pale vertical shafts in the nave wall look like missing or transparent pieces.
- The nave roof is an obvious stamp repetition, with identical highlight tiles in a regular grid.
- The porch gable roof overlaps the nave roof with an ambiguous junction (no valley or ridge), so it does not read as one coherent massing.

VERDICT: FAIL
